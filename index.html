Introduction

         Nuclear fusion has begun to establish itself as a safe, economical, and unlimited energy 
         source [1] in the modern world. The purpose of this article is to focus on Tokamak fusion 
         reactors, several of the technologies that make the tokamak fusion process possible in 
         the first place, as well as some of the problems faced in current research on tokamak 
         fusion. Designs of tokamaks are highly variable, but put simply, a tokamak is a toroidal
         (roughly donut) shaped mechanism designed to superheat plasma in a vacuum to heat 
         up a lithium ‘blanket’, evaporating water (more on this later), which turns a turbine to 
         produce energy.
